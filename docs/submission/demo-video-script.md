# Narrated demo script — target 2:45

> Private recording draft. Keep the final public upload at or below three
> minutes, with clear spoken narration. Record only after the final candidate is
> deployed and reverified.

## 0:00–0:18 — The problem

**Screen:** Sky to Porch landing page and empty map.

**Narration:**

“Official weather and environmental data is available, but answering a
personal question still means finding the right source, place, time, and
limitations. Sky to Porch lets a person and an AI agent inspect that evidence
together on the same map.”

## 0:18–0:42 — Ask the real question

**Screen:** In the supported WebMCP Agent surface, enter:

> Could Hurricane Beryl have damaged my home or roof in Houston on July 8,
> 2024, and what official environmental evidence can help me discuss it with my
> insurer?

**Narration:**

“I ask the concern directly. The Agent calls the environmental analysis tool,
which uses the same validated controller as the human form. It updates the
place, date, selected map area, Meaning, Evidence, and a visible Agent receipt.”

## 0:42–1:18 — Lead with evidence

**Screen:** Show the Meaning panel, then the strongest Wind observations and
citations. Keep the 39.6 m/s gust and 25.7 m/s wind speed legible.

**Narration:**

“The result leads with what was actually observed: a peak gust of 39.6 meters
per second and peak wind speed of 25.7 meters per second near Houston, with the
observation time and links to NOAA and the National Weather Service. The
assessment is moderate confidence because regional observations can support a
discussion, but they cannot establish damage to one roof.”

## 1:18–1:42 — Related context without conflation

**Screen:** Scroll to the separately labelled Flood & Heavy Rain section and
its NASA citation.

**Narration:**

“Because this is a broad storm-impact question, Sky to Porch also checks Flood
and Heavy Rain. Wind and water remain independent evidence chains. The product
never turns rainfall or flood imagery into proof of wind damage, and it labels
the combined view as co-occurring context, not causation.”

## 1:42–2:05 — Contextual tools

**Screen:** Have the Agent inspect the current evidence; show the exact value,
time, and citations without rerunning the query. Then invoke the storm
discussion tool.

**Narration:**

“Once a result exists, a new inspection tool becomes available and reads the
current validated evidence without querying again. Because this result is Home
plus Wind, a second contextual tool appears and opens a focused discussion
guide.”

## 2:05–2:27 — Human next steps, bounded claims

**Screen:** Show the checklist and questions for photos, inspection timing,
pre-existing condition, and policy discussion.

**Narration:**

“The guide helps organize property-specific evidence and questions for an
insurer. It does not decide damage, causation, coverage, liability, or a claim
outcome. Those boundaries stay visible to both the person and the Agent.”

## 2:27–2:45 — Why WebMCP

**Screen:** Brief split view of the Agent receipt, map, and evidence panel; end
on the product name and live URL.

**Narration:**

“WebMCP makes the browser page a shared, inspectable workspace instead of a
separate API. Dynamic tools expose only actions that fit the current result,
while deterministic code owns evidence, provenance, confidence, and safety.
That is Sky to Porch: official evidence, from the sky to your porch.”

## Recording checklist

- Keep observation values, dates, confidence, and source links readable.
